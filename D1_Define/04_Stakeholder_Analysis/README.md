# 👥 Stakeholder Analysis & Engagement Plan
**Project:** Madar-Retail Improvement | **Date:** 06/05/2026

## 📌 Overview
This document outlines the identification, analysis, and engagement strategy for all stakeholders involved in the Madar-Retail project. Managing these relationships is critical to ensuring project buy-in and mitigating operational risks.

---

## 1. Stakeholder Identification & Roles
We have identified 6 key stakeholders across internal and external categories.

| Stakeholder | Type | Primary Role | Influence Area |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Associates** | Internal | Direct customer interaction | Engagement, Upselling, Satisfaction |
| **Store Manager** | Internal | Oversees operations & KPIs | Staff management, Process control |
| **Inventory/Supply Chain** | Internal | Controls product availability | Stock levels, Replenishment |
| **Customers** | External | End users & revenue drivers | Purchase decisions, NPS |
| **Brand/Vendor** | External | Provides products & pricing | Supply reliability, Assortment |
| **Finance Team** | Internal | Sets pricing & budgets | ROI, Margins, Budgeting |

---

## 2. Power, Interest, and Impact Matrix
Understanding the weight of each stakeholder helps prioritize communication efforts.

*   **High Power / High Interest:** Store Manager (Must win first).
*   **Medium Power / High Impact:** Sales Associates (Who matter most for conversion).
*   **High Power / Silent Risk:** Finance Team (Can kill the project via budget freeze).

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest | Impact | Attitude |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Store Manager | High | High | High | Support |
| Sales Associates | Medium | High | High | Support |
| Finance Team | High | Medium | Medium | Neutral |
| Customers | Low | High | High | Support |

---

## 3. Conflict & CTQ Alignment
Different stakeholders care about different outcomes, which can lead to conflicts.

*   **The "Price vs. Margin" Conflict:** Customers want lower prices, while Finance wants margin protection.
*   **The "Inventory" Conflict:** Store Managers want high SKU availability, while Finance wants to lower inventory holding costs.
*   **The "Workload" Conflict:** Sales associates care about manageable workloads, which may conflict with Finance if bonuses are tied to uncontrolled metrics.

---

## 4. Engagement & Communication Strategy
To keep the project on track, we follow a structured communication cadence.

| Stakeholder | Frequency | Communication Channel | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Associates** | Weekly | Team meetings & briefings | Store Manager |
| **Store Manager** | Bi-weekly | 1:1 meetings & Dashboards | Project Lead |
| **Inventory/Supply Chain** | Monthly | Email & joint planning | Store Manager |
| **Finance Team** | Monthly | Financial reports & presentations | Project Lead |

---

## 5. Risk & Mitigation
| Stakeholder | Risk to Project | Risk Level | Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Associates** | Resistance to new training | Medium | Link training to incentives; involve in design |
| **Inventory** | Stockouts undermine efforts | High | Set joint stockout reduction targets |
| **Customers** | Permanent shift to online | High | Fast-track staff engagement changes |
| **Finance** | Budget rejection | Medium | Present ROI case; start with low-cost pilot |

---

## 💡 Strategic Summary
The **Store Manager** is the most critical ally to win first, as their buy-in unlocks staff access. However, the **Finance Team** and **Supply Chain** represent the highest operational risks if their specific concerns (costs and stockouts) are not addressed early in the process.
